Design options for your portfolio

The new designs you asked for are at the top: four built around your photo, then designs 3 and 4 recoloured in the blue from design 2. The seven earlier designs are still below. Each picture is the opening screen of one design. Press Open live preview to try the real page, and tell Claude the number you like.

New: built around your photo

All four take their deep blue from the studio backdrop in your pictures. Three use the barong portrait as the main image, because it is the more formal of the two. The graduation photo shows your old school's name on the sash, so it is cropped close or used small.

8

Lanyard badge

Your photo sits on a developer badge that hangs from a lanyard on a bright blue page. The badge swings in when the page loads, tilts as the mouse moves, and flips over when clicked.

Open live preview

Why choose it

The bluest of the set, friendly, and honest about being a student. The badge is a small moment people remember.

Trade-off

Hanging badges are a current trend on developer sites, so other portfolios use the idea too.

9

Magazine cover Claude's pick

The page is the cover of a magazine named after you. Your portrait is the cover photo, the title runs across the top in blue, and your four projects are the cover headlines.

Open live preview

Why choose it

It looks the most professional, your photo is the centre of attention, and all four projects are on the first screen.

Trade-off

Magazine-style portfolios are a familiar idea. Scroll down in the live preview to see the profile page that uses your graduation photo.

10

Blue record sleeve

Styled like a classic jazz album cover: your photo tinted entirely in blue on one side, your name in heavy type on the other, and your projects listed as tracks.

Open live preview

Why choose it

Striking and very blue. The tint turns an ordinary studio photo into a design element, and hovering a track swaps in that project's screenshot.

Trade-off

The close crop shows your face very large. It uses the smiling graduation photo; the barong photo can be swapped in.

11

Portrait standard

The usual Awwwards portrait layout, done cleanly: your photo fading into a dark navy page, your name in very large type across it, and the project list to the side.

Open live preview

Why choose it

Safe, elegant and quick to understand. This is what most award-winning personal sites with a photo look like.

Trade-off

It is the least original of the four, so it stands out only through polish and motion.

Earlier: bold concepts

Each of these turns the whole site into one idea. They are the most memorable, and the least like other portfolios.

1

Sliding puzzle

The home page is a sliding puzzle. Its eight tiles are the sections of your site, and it solves itself with the same A* search you wrote for Pathfinder-8.

Open live preview

Why choose it

Nobody else can copy it, because it runs on your own project. A client sees working code in the first second.

Trade-off

It is playful. The puzzle has to solve itself quickly so visitors are not kept waiting.

2

Build instructions

Each project is drawn like a step in a brick instruction booklet, showing the parts it is made of: a React brick on an Express brick on a PostgreSQL base.

Open live preview

Why choose it

It explains what each project is built from at a glance, and everyone recognises the booklet.

Trade-off

The toy look could feel too childish for some employers.

3

Network map

Your four projects are four coloured transit lines. They cross at the skills they share, such as JavaScript and SQL databases.

Open live preview

Why choose it

It shows your range across four kinds of software in one picture.

Trade-off

Skill maps drawn as subway lines have been done before, and visitors do not see project screenshots until they click.

4

Card album

Your projects are numbered collector cards in an album. Four are printed, four slots are still empty. Click a card and it flips to show its details.

Open live preview

Why choose it

The back of each card is a neat place for the technical details a client checks.

Trade-off

It can look like a hobby site, and the empty slots draw attention to how few projects there are.

Earlier: calmer options

5

Four posters

Each project is a tall colour poster with its name in huge type. Point at a poster and it widens to show a screenshot and description.

Open live preview

6

Gallery wall

Your projects hang as framed pictures on a white gallery wall, each with a small museum label. Visitors scroll sideways along the wall.

Open live preview

Why choose it

7

Awwwards standard

Black and white with very large type. A list of your projects, a preview image that follows the mouse, and your full name across the bottom.

Open live preview

Why choose it

This is the look of recent Awwwards Site of the Day winners such as jesperlandberg.com and twks.ch.

Trade-off

It looks like many other portfolios, so it stands out only through polish and motion.
